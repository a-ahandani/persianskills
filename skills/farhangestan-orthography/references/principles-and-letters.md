# Principles, spacing types and letters (book pp. 7–33)

Source: فرهنگستان زبان و ادب فارسی، «دستور خطّ فارسی (ویراست جدید)»، چاپ اوّل
۱۴۰۱. Page numbers are the printed book pages, which match the PDF's pages.
Rules below are summarized in our own words. "Our reading" marks an
interpretation that the book does not state.

## What this edition changed (pp. 17–20)

- The first edition fixed only the cases where joined or separate writing was
  mandatory and left the rest to taste. The new edition sets rules for spacing
  in compounds: words with prefixes and suffixes, and compound words (p. 18).
- For compound spacing, follow this edition (pp. 34–42, 58–71), not habits
  from the old one. The reprinted first-edition introduction (pp. 7–14) warns
  that some of its examples may no longer match (p. 13, footnote).
- Out of scope (pp. 11–13): punctuation, writing foreign words in Persian
  script, romanization, poetry (rules are written for prose) and calligraphy.

## The seven criteria (pp. 21–22)

These explain the rules; they are not edits to apply directly.

1. Keep the familiar look of the script, so older texts stay readable.
2. Persian spelling does not follow Arabic spelling, except that Quranic verses
   and quoted Arabic passages keep their original form (REQUIRED).
3. Spelling follows pronunciation as far as the script allows.
4. Rules have as few exceptions as possible, and exceptions are closed lists.
   A word not on an exception list follows the general rule.
5. A rule should not depend on the word's meaning or context where avoidable.
6. Rules should be easy to learn and apply.
7. Spacing matters, because wrong spacing causes misreading.

## The three spacing types (pp. 22–23)

| Type | When | Examples from the book |
|---|---|---|
| فاصلهٔ کامل (full space) | between independent words and phrases | «پژوهشکدهٔ واژه‌گزینی» |
| نیم‌فاصله (ZWNJ, U+200C) | parts of a compound that the rules keep separate, when the first part ends in a joining letter | «می‌آیم»، «جابه‌جا»، «وسیع‌تر»، «هم‌محل»، «شست‌وشو» |
| بی‌فاصله (nothing) | the same separate-writing case when the first part ends in a non-joining letter (ا د ذ ر ز ژ و, or silent final ه) | «هوادار»، «پرفروش»، «مادربزرگ»، «روسیاه» |

- نیم‌فاصله and بی‌فاصله are one outcome, "write the parts separately". The last
  letter of the first part decides which one applies. The book's phrase for
  this throughout is «نیم‌فاصله/ بی‌فاصله (بسته به نوع حروف)».
- The book says a ZWNJ is not needed after a non-joining letter (p. 23). Our
  reading: treat no ZWNJ as the standard there, and remove a stray one only as
  normalization.
- "Separate" is a different outcome from «پیوسته» (fully joined, no ZWNJ).

## Joining letters (pp. 26–27)

- Non-joining (منفصل): ا، د، ذ، ر، ز، ژ، و. They never join the next letter.
- All other letters join (متّصل).
- Silent final ه (های غیرملفوظ) counts as non-joining for spacing, though ه is
  a joining letter (p. 27, footnote). So «خانه» + suffix behaves like a word
  ending in ا or د.

## Sounds, letters and vowels (pp. 24–28)

- Several letters for one sound: glottal stop ء/ع; t ت/ط; s ث/س/ص; h ح/ه;
  z ذ/ز/ض/ظ; gh غ/ق. Which letter a word takes is lexical; see the
  multi-spelling lists (pp. 73–85) for words with accepted variants.
- One letter for several sounds: و (short o «دو», long u «روز», v «وام»,
  ow «روشن»); ی (y «یار», long i «میز», ā in «موسی»، «حتّی»); ه (h «هوا»,
  vowel e «نامه»).
- «واو معدوله» after خ is written but not pronounced: «خواهر»، «خویش»، «خوان».
- Short vowels are usually not written, so homographs exist («برد»). Where
  leaving them out makes the reading ambiguous, especially foreign names, the
  word should be vowel-marked (p. 26): «بِکِت»، «سارْتْر»، «مَلِک، مَلَک، مُلْک، مِلْک».
- Arabic words, phrases and expressions used unchanged keep Arabic spelling
  (REQUIRED, p. 27): «اصلاً»، «بالقوّه»، «بقیّةاللّه»، «حتّی»، «موسی». Details on pp. 54–55.
- Initial hamza is written on alef, shaped by the vowel after it: اَ، اِ، اُ، آ،
  او، ای (p. 24, footnote 2): «ابر»، «اسم»، «استاد»، «آرد»، «او»، «ایران».

## Table 1: the 33 primary signs (pp. 29–32)

- Order: ء ا ب پ ت ث ج چ ح خ د ذ ر ز ژ س ش ص ض ط ظ ع غ ف ق ک گ ل م ن و ه ی.
- «آ» is treated as a letter of its own and sorts first, as in current
  dictionaries (p. 29, footnote 1).
- «ة» is an Arabic-only variant kept in a few Arabic compounds such as
  «دایرةالمعارف»; it is not a Persian letter (p. 29, footnote 2).
- Hamza seat forms: ئـ ـئـ (initial or medial on a tooth), ـأ ـؤ ـئ (joined to
  the previous letter only), ء and أ (joined to neither): «رئیس»، «لئیم»،
  «مأخذ»، «مؤمن»، «متلألئ»، «جزء»، «رأس». Rules are on pp. 49–53.
- The table shows only Persian «ک» and «ی» (dotless at the end of a word).
  Our reading: use U+06A9 and U+06CC, never Arabic ك and ي.

## Table 2: the 10 secondary signs (p. 33)

madd «آ»; fatha, damma, kasra; sukun; tashdid; the small ی on silent ه «ـهٔ»
(«نامهٔ من»); tanvin nasb «ـاً» («ظاهراً»), rafʿ («مضافٌ‌الیه») and jarr
(«بعبارةٍاُخری»).

- Fatha, damma, kasra, sukun and tashdid are optional and serve to remove
  ambiguity (BOTH ALLOWED, p. 33). Don't add or strip them except for that.
- Tanvin, tashdid in homographs and the ezafe sign have their own rules
  (pp. 47–48, 56–57).

## Notes on reading these examples

The PDF's text layer is broken, so examples were transcribed from the page
images. ZWNJ positions were judged from the letter shapes: a joining letter in
its final form inside a word means a ZWNJ follows it.
